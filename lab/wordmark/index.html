00Wordmark lab · temporary page

A wordmark drawn in the Actus grammar

The new Actualise AI wordmark is built exactly the way the AI mark is built: one even stroke on a unit grid, corners turning on a single radius, flat ends, and bars that lie over the stroke with a hairline where they cross. The wordmark and the mark become one family, and because the letters are drawn rather than set in a font, there is no licence to buy.

01Principles

What good lettering does, and how it's applied here

Three habits from type design, adapted to a single-line grammar.

Overshoot

Round edges usually go slightly past the baseline and cap line so they look as tall as flat ones. With the mark's tight one-unit corners, only a sliver of each letter is round, so the correction would be invisible; the grid stays exact, as in the mark.

Overshoot (typography)Tobias Frere-Jones on the illusion

Balance inside each letter

Type designers thin horizontals and move bars off the true middle so letters look even. Here the stroke stays even, true to the mark, and the balance comes from placement instead: the A's bar sits where the mark's does, the E's bar is shorter than its arms, and the S turns at the middle of the grid.

Optical corrections for logo designersOptical vs mathematical alignment

Spacing by shape

Letters are spaced by their sides, not by equal gaps: straight sides get the most room, curves a little less, open sides (T, and the right of C, L and E) far less. That evens out the white space between letters, which matters more than the letters themselves in a wide-set wordmark.

02The grammar

Taken from the AI mark

Five rules, read straight off the mark, and applied to every letter.

Unit
One stroke wide. Every letter sits on a grid of that unit, seven units tall, as the mark does
Stroke
Even, everywhere. No thick and thin: the letters are drawn with a single line
Corners
One radius. Every turn is a quarter circle one unit across its centre line, so the outside reads soft and the inside tight
Ends
Flat and square. Strokes stop cleanly, never rounded
Crossings
Bars lie over the stroke. Where one does, a hairline in the ground colour shows the crossing. Used sparingly: only where the mark uses it, in the A and the I

03Letters

Eight letters, one line each

Each letter on its grid. The thin line is the centre of the stroke; where two lines meet, the bar lies on top. C, U, L, S and E are each a single turning stroke; A and E add a bar; the I is the mark's own I.

04How much hairline

Sprinkled, not scattered

The hairline is the mark's signature, so it has to be used with restraint. Three amounts, tried side by side.

Recommended · only in the final AIthe word ends the way the mark looks

Everywhere a bar crossesA, E, A and I

The mark's own AI, connectedthe A turns into the I

Recommended: hairlines only in the final AI. The word reads cleanly, and the last two letters quietly repeat the symbol. Everywhere is too busy. The connected AI is striking, but it turns the end of the word into a logo inside a logo; it's worth keeping for special uses.

05Spacing

Spaced by shape

The shaded areas are each letter's side spacing, in grid units: straight sides 1, the curves of C, S and E a little less, bar ends 0.6, and the open sides of T, L, C and E much less, so the white space between letters evens out.

07Earlier exploration

Where it started: a geometric frame

The first attempt built the letters from a rounded frame with a larger radius, thinner bars and optical overshoot. It was even and well spaced, but generic: it could have belonged to any tech brand. Building from the mark's own grammar is what made it ours.
