00Wordmark lab · temporary page

A wordmark drawn in the Actus grammar

The new Actualise AI wordmark is built the way the AI mark is built: one even stroke on a unit grid, corners on a single radius, flat ends, and bars that lie over the stroke with a hairline where they cross. Then it is refined by eye, because a strict grid is not the same as visual balance. Drawn, not set in a font, so there is no licence to buy.

Drawn the way the AI mark draws: the line first, then the bars, then the hairlines

01Adjust the spacing

Kern it yourself

Every gap between letters, in grid units (one unit is one stroke). Drag a slider or type a value; the wordmark updates as you go. Flip it upside down or blur it to judge the white rather than the letters. Your settings are saved in this browser; when you're happy, copy them and paste them to me, and I'll bake them into the artwork.

Suggested values come from the type-design method: 3.0 between two straight sides, less where a side is open (T, the right of C, L and E, both sides of I), never so tight that the tips touch.

02The hairline

Centred on the edge, or outlining the shape

Where a bar lies over the main stroke, a hairline marks the crossing. Centred on the stroke's edge (left), it eats into the main shape as well as the bar. As an outline (right), it sits just outside the main stroke and cuts only into the added-on bar, so the A, the turn and the I stay whole and the bars read as laid on top.

04The grammar

Taken from the AI mark

Five rules, read straight off the mark and applied to every letter.

Unit
One stroke wide. Every letter sits on a grid of that unit, seven units tall, as the mark does
Stroke
Even, everywhere. No thick and thin: each letter is drawn with one line, which is also what lets it be animated like the mark
Corners
One radius. Every turn is a quarter circle one unit across its centre line: soft outside, tight inside
Ends
Flat and square. Strokes stop cleanly
Crossings
Bars lie over the stroke, and a hairline in the ground colour shows the crossing, used only where the mark uses it: the final A and I

05Refined by eye

Off the grid where the eye needs it

A strict grid gives every letter the same logic, but not the same visual weight: open letters look bigger, bars leave holes, and a symmetrical S looks top-heavy. Each letter below is nudged off the grid by fractions of a unit. Solid: refined. Outline: the strict-grid letter. Thin line: the centre of the stroke.

A · 5 units wide

Unchanged: the mark's own A, so the word and the symbol share it exactly.

C · 4.6 units wide

0.4 narrower, with the top arm a touch shorter than the bottom, so it sits firmly and its open side doesn't leave a hole before the T.

T · 3.9 units wide

Bar 1.1 shorter, narrower than the C and the E: the T has the biggest voids of any letter, so its shape is fixed before its spacing.

U · 5.3 units wide

0.3 wider than the A: with no bar and an open top, a U the same width as the A reads narrow beside it.

L · 3.7 units wide

Foot 0.8 shorter, so the open space above it doesn't punch a gap before the I.

I · 3.5 units wide

The mark's I, with bars 0.5 narrower inside the word, so its open sides sit closer to the L and S; the AI mark at the end keeps its own proportions.

S · 4.8 units wide

The top bowl is set in and the spine raised slightly, so the S stands steady rather than looking top-heavy.

E · 4.4 units wide

0.1 narrower; the middle bar is shorter than the arms and sits a little above centre, the optical middle.
