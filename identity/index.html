Identity: the Actus system

A symbol, a pattern and a set of rules, built from one word and the wordmark Actualise AI already has.

Secondary mark

For app icons, favicons, avatars and sign-offs, where the name is already known.

The idea

Actualise comes from the Latin actus: a doing, and the run of a plough before it turns.

The run

One clear pass of work. That is Speed to Execution™, the thing Actualise already promises.

The turn

Every turn lands on the next run and builds on it. As one client put it: after a few iterations.

The yoke

A Roman field was ploughed by a yoke of oxen. The person sets the line; AI is the yoke that lets the same day cover more ground.

Runs and turns make a field. A Roman actus was the length an ox team ploughed before turning: 120 feet. Everything in this identity is built from that one drawing and its rules.

The symbol

The plough writes AI.

One furrow

One line, never lifted. It climbs the A, turns across the top, comes down, runs along the ground to the I, and climbs into its top bar.

The crossings

The A’s crossbar and the I’s bars are straight runs. The furrow ploughs across them, and a fine hairline shows it passing over. It is the same detail as the wordmark.

Built on the wordmark’s grid

One band width, one turn radius equal to the band, a cap height of seven bands. The construction drawing shows the grid.

Rules

Punk mode · modern punk

A gig poster, not a brochure

Punk design came from its tools and its attitude: make it yourself, say it plainly. Punk mode borrows that method rather than 1977 iconography: a black ground, acid and hot pink as flat fills, hard-edged cards with offset shadows, high-contrast duotone photos, stickers, and one cut-out headline per page. On the homepage a piece of AI hype is crossed out, with the plain version scrawled over it. Motion is stepped and brief.

The rules: fluoro colours are fills or large type, body copy stays level and plain, cut-out letters are hidden from screen readers (which read the heading’s plain text), and grain and motion switch off with reduced motion or high contrast.
