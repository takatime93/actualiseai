Identity: Actus

One grammar for everything: the AI mark, the wordmark and the field are all drawn with the same line, on the same grid, with the same crossings.

Secondary mark

For app icons, favicons, avatars and sign-offs, where the name is already known.

The idea

Actualise comes from the Latin actus: a doing, and the run of a plough before it turns.

The run

One clear pass of work. That is Speed to Execution™, the thing Actualise already promises.

The turn

Every turn lands on the next run and builds on it. As one client put it: after a few iterations.

The yoke

A Roman field was ploughed by a yoke of oxen. The person sets the line; AI is the yoke that lets the same day cover more ground.

Runs and turns make a field. A Roman actus was the length an ox team ploughed before turning: 120 feet. Everything in this identity is built from that one drawing and its rules.

The symbol

The plough writes AI.

One furrow

One line, never lifted. It climbs the A, turns across the top, comes down, runs along the ground to the I, and climbs into its top bar.

The crossings

The A’s crossbar and the I’s bars are straight runs. The furrow ploughs across them, and a fine hairline, cut out of the shape, shows it passing over. The wordmark ends with this same AI.

Built on the wordmark’s grid

One band width, one turn radius equal to the band, a cap height of seven bands. The construction drawing shows the grid.

Actualise and AI

The old meaning and the new technology are the same line.

The wordmark

Drawn in the same grammar as the AI mark.

Not set in a font: every letter is one even stroke on a seven-unit grid, turning on a single radius, with flat ends. Bars lie over the stroke, and the word ends with the AI mark itself.

Line first, then the bars, then the hairlines

A · 5 units wide

Unchanged: the mark's own A, so the word and the symbol share it exactly.

C · 4.6 units wide

0.4 narrower, with the top arm a touch shorter than the bottom, so it sits firmly and its open side doesn't leave a hole before the T.

T · 3.9 units wide

Bar 1.1 shorter, narrower than the C and the E: the T has the biggest voids of any letter, so its shape is fixed before its spacing.

U · 5.3 units wide

0.3 wider than the A: with no bar and an open top, a U the same width as the A reads narrow beside it.

L · 3.7 units wide

Foot 0.8 shorter, so the open space above it doesn't punch a gap before the I.

I · 3.5 units wide

The mark's I, with bars 0.5 narrower inside the word, so its open sides sit closer to the L and S; the AI mark at the end keeps its own proportions.

S · 4.8 units wide

The top bowl is set in and the spine raised slightly, so the S stands steady rather than looking top-heavy.

E · 4.4 units wide

0.1 narrower; the middle bar is shorter than the arms and sits a little above centre, the optical middle.

One stroke

Each letter is drawn with one line, the same width everywhere. That is also why the wordmark can draw itself like the mark.

Refined by eye

The grid gives every letter the same logic; small adjustments give them the same weight. The T bar and L foot are shorter, the U a little wider, and the S's top bowl is set in.

Spaced by hand

Each gap set by eye in grid units: {kern}.

Pure shapes

The hairlines are real gaps in the artwork, so it works on any colour, photo or texture. No licence: it is original artwork, so it can be registered as a trade mark.

The rule

The symbol already says AI, so it never sits beside the wordmark. Use the wordmark for the name, and the symbol for app icons, favicons, avatars and sign-offs. Minimum sizes: symbol 16 px tall, wordmark 140 px wide.

Motion

Everything is ploughed, never faded in.

The symbol

The furrow draws in one pass from the foot of the A to the end of the I, then the crossings follow. About two seconds.

The wordmark

Drawn the same way, letter by letter, left to right: each line first, then its bars, then the hairlines. About two seconds.

The field

The field is one furrow too. It draws up and down, run by run, then the crossings follow.

Where

Site load, video, the end of a presentation, an app’s loading state. In use it draws once and holds; here it loops so you can watch it.

In use

The same rules, from the homepage to the boardroom.

Next steps

Four steps before the identity goes public.

  1. 01

    Clearance

    A trademark search on the wordmark, the symbol and the field. The wordmark is original artwork, so there is no font licence to buy.

  2. 02

    Master artwork

    Lock the drawn wordmark and its kerning, and export the master files: wordmark, AI mark and lockups, as pure shapes.

  3. 03

    Motion files

    Export the drawing animation, line first, then bars, then hairlines, as video and Lottie.

    A brand guide covering the wordmark's construction, logo use, colour, type, the field, and templates.
